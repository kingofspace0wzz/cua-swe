This inbox simulates a foreman's external delivery in an isolated browser. Outside the prepared review runtime the inbox is empty. Attachments are original drawings, not the live computed timeline. The three sheets are overlapping horizontal sections of one wall chart, with repeated row names and registration crosses. Overlap repeats the same ink: never count a dependency twice.

Measure and trace

  1. Choose a sheet, then Read at 85%. Pan using drag, scroll, or arrow buttons. Read all sheets. Every fifth day line is numbered; count the intervening day intervals. Physical line spacing is hand-ruled and not perfectly even. Every bar edge is exactly on a day rule. The left edge is planned start; the number of intervals to the right edge is duration. Do not count both boundary lines as days.
  2. Follow each arrow to its tip, not merely to where another line crosses it. A little arched bridge at a crossing means no junction. Connections occur only at bar edges; repeated source/destination bars may have separate ports along that edge. Arrow routes do not encode time.
  3. FS: solid line, one filled tip; leaves a bar's right (finish) edge, arrives at a left (start) edge. SS: dashed line, open triangular tip; leaves and arrives at left edges. FF: solid line, double tip; leaves and arrives at right edges. Lag: count short strokes in the little circle on the line. No circle means zero lag. All links use nonnegative whole-day lag.

At 85% on a 400×800 CSS-pixel phone (DPR 1), the narrowest day interval is 27.2 device pixels, bars are 40.8 high, arrow strokes about 2.55, arrowheads 9.35 long by 10.2 high, bridges 11.9 across, lag strokes 8.5 high with 5.95 spacing. Separate parallel arrow centrelines are at least 13.6 apart (at least 10.4 clear ink pixels, allowing for hand wobble). Crossings and true bar attachments are the only exceptions. Zoom in for another look; no pinch required.

Compute, don't substitute the planned dates

Use each job's measured duration and incoming arrows. Earliest starts are at least the chart's first day. For a link from A to B with lag L: FS requires start(B) ≥ start(A)+duration(A)+L. SS requires start(B) ≥ start(A)+L. FF requires start(B) ≥ start(A)+duration(A)+L−duration(B). Earliest finish is the largest earliest start+duration. Planned dates are not lower bounds on this calculation.

A planned violation is a job drawn earlier than at least one incoming link allows, using the other bars' planned dates in these same formulas (or earlier than the first day). Latest starts are the latest feasible starts that keep earliest finish fixed. Float is latest minus earliest; zero-float jobs are critical. Critical paths are maximal chains of tight links between zero-float jobs, including a lone critical job when applicable. Ties produce multiple paths, not an arbitrary winner.

Slip preview holds the selected job no earlier than its baseline earliest start + slip days and recalculates finish. Slip is not extra duration and does not rewrite planned dates. Other jobs propagate through the same link rules. The report shows the moved finish and its difference from baseline finish.

Draft review contract

Apply job edit changes start or duration. Dependency controls add, remove, or retype incoming links; order is irrelevant. All changes and slip previews update the live schedule, complete ledger and report immediately. A dependency cycle or malformed integration gives a plain-language error; the inbox, guide and received chart stay usable.

Undo restores one draft action. Cancel restores the last saved schedule and slip; Save stores them independently for the selected edition. Notes save independently as you type. Ordinary reload preserves saved schedules and notes, and discards unsaved drafts; changing editions does the same. Reset restores this edition's received integration, clearing its saved draft/slip but preserving notes and the other edition. Export draft opens an inline, complete human-readable snapshot of the current draft (including links, report, slip and notes). It does not download a file.
