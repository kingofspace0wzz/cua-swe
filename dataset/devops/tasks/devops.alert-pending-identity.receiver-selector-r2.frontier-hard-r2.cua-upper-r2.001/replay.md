# Replay

Open the console at its assigned URL. The initial series remains above the shown
threshold through 3:00, longer than the 120-second hold, yet the alert timeline
stays pending with a fresh active-since time and fingerprint at each evaluation.
The delivery line shows the receiver, its constant match selector, and the
routing epoch context: every epoch scope carries a short bracketed policy class
code; one scope's pair rotates at every Next evaluation and returns to its
initial value after Replay capture or Reset, while the other scopes' pairs stay
fixed within a capture. Open Labels & detail: two expanded labels churn between
evaluations — one tracks the plotted sample and one tracks the rotating epoch
value — while service, instance, severity, team, zone, the routed selector
label, and the capture-stable epoch labels stay constant. Correlating the churn
with the rotating epoch pair supports identity misplacement of per-evaluation
context in the rule adapter rather than insufficient time, a threshold miss, a
restart, or a receiver outage.

The class codes themselves are opaque. Their custody meaning is published only
on the Routing runbook tab, a posted raster operations sheet available before
any configuration: two classes are durable receiver identity and two classes —
including one whose epoch pair holds perfectly still between evaluations — are
descriptive detail only. The rule catalog declares exactly one class per epoch
scope; the runbook is the only place those classes are defined.

Repair the adapter so enrichment templates that reference the sample value or a
descriptive-class routing epoch key become annotations, while static,
series-derived, and durable-class epoch enrichments remain identity labels.
Gold binds the catalog epoch declaration into rule compilation and encodes the
complete runbook custody mapping as a durable local policy, without requesting
a snapshot before configure. The same samples then fire after the full hold and
delivery counts every firing alert. Short burst remains healthy after recovery.
Independent targets keep their own pending clocks. Advance, Replay capture,
Reset, and browser reload remain deterministic, and the runbook sheet stays
posted byte-exactly.

Protected profiles rotate epoch scope and key names, epoch value formats,
rotation cadence (including rotation every second evaluation), enrichment names
and order, value scales, hold/interval, and target names, while every profile
uses all four policy classes. Consuming rule-family ownership (absent from the
catalog), requesting snapshot before configure, moving only $value templates,
moving every $route template, treating every capture-stable class as durable,
hard-coding one class or the visible enrichment names, deleting enrichments, or
repainting the epoch line, route table, or runbook sheet all fail at least one
profile. Baseline failure, gold replay, and construction probes are not model
results.
